# Deep dive: physical chemistry of pMHC recognition — water, electrostatics, dynamics, empty MHC — for design against KRAS G12D / HLA-A*11:01

Prepared 2026-10-30. Read-only web research; every literature finding carries a URL. Items marked **[abstract-only]** were only available as abstracts; items marked **[computed]** are my own measurements from the deposited coordinates named in-line (URLs given). Quotes are verbatim from the source text.

Context of the target: decamer peptide VVVGADGVGK (KRAS G12D, P6 = Asp) vs WT VVVGAGGVGK presented by HLA-A*11:01 + β2m; first-pass conclusion already established that Asp6 is ~90% buried, salt-bridged to HLA Arg114 (stabilised by D116, plus a Q70 H-bond), and that discrimination by the known JDI TCR is indirect/electrostatic.

## 1. Water at pMHC and TCR–pMHC interfaces

### 1.1 Structured water in the peptide-binding groove

- **63 conserved water positions in MHC-I (cluster analysis of 12 high-resolution structures).** Ogata & Wodak identified "a total of 63 clusters, corresponding to water molecules, whose positions are conserved in half or more of the analyzed structures"; "The most highly conserved of the three appears to play an important role in stabilizing the conformation of a twisted beta-turn between residues 118 and 122"; "The third highly conserved water molecule is located at the first kink of the alpha2 helix, possibly playing a role in determining the position of the N-terminal segment of that helix, which also carries side chains in contact with the bound peptide." — Ogata & Wodak, *Protein Eng.* 15(8):697-705 (2002). **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12364585/
- **Water actively assists peptide binding in HLA-A2 (MD).** Petrone & Garcia (5 ns MD of HLA-A2 ± the HIV RT epitope ILKEPVHGV) "identified 14 water molecules that stay bound for periods longer than 1ns in regions previously identified by crystallography. These water molecules in the interface perform definite 'tasks' contributing to the binding energy: hydrogen bond bridges between MHC and peptide and filling empty spaces in the groove which enhance affinity without contributing to epitope specificity." They "find that there is an overall gain in free energy resulting from the formation of water clusters at the epitope-MHC interface. Water molecules serving the task of filling empty spaces bind at the interface with a net gain in entropy, relative to their entropy in bulk." Conclusion: "water molecules at the interface play the role of active mediators in the MHC-peptide interaction, and might be responsible for the large binding affinity of the MHC complex to a large number of epitope sequences." — Petrone & Garcia, *J. Mol. Biol.* 338(2):419-35 (2004). **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15066441/

### 1.2 Water in TCR recognition — direct thermodynamic evidence

- **Different TCRs use interfacial water differently (osmolyte/dehydration experiments).** Anikeeva et al. compared D3 TCR (HIV peptide/HLA-A2) vs 2B4 TCR: "osmolytic reagents that dehydrate protein surfaces have profoundly different effects on the strength of the two reactions, indicating that water molecules make very different contributions-enhancing the binding of D3 TCR but weakening the binding of 2B4 TCR. The use of these different mechanisms by TCRs to recognize ligands might be an important means augmenting their inherent cross-reactivity." Both reactions were "consistent with a conformational adjustment at the binding interface during the formation of specific TCR-peptide-MHC complexes." — Anikeeva et al., *Biochemistry* 42(19):5594-602 (2003). **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12705834/
- **Interfacial waters in high-resolution TCR–pMHC structures bridge and 'neutralize unpaired hydrogen-bonding groups'.** In the p53 study below (Wu et al. 2022): "in several high resolution TCR–pMHC structures (≤2.5 Å), interfacial waters have been found to form bridging hydrogen bonds that enhance polar interactions and neutralize unpaired hydrogen-bonding groups" (their ref. 28 = Yang, Chen, Weng & Mariuzza, *J. Biol. Chem.* 292:18618-18627, 2017). Their own 3.33 Å 6-11 complex "does not permit the identification of ordered waters with confidence, and none were included in the final model." — https://www.jbc.org/article/S0021-9258(22)00124-7/fulltext ; ref 28: https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M117.810382
- **Trapped water in TCR–pMHC cavities may contribute to complex stability.** Golovin et al. (Sykulev lab), *Biophys. J.* 124(7):1073-1084 (2025): the article notes "water molecules that are trapped in the cavities at the TCR-pMHC interface could also contribute to the stability of the TCR-pMHC complex". The same paper reports: "Supercomputer simulations and a well-tempered metadynamics approach revealed several metastable noncanonical TCR-pMHC interactions that depend on the conformation of the MHC-bound peptides. The diversity of metastable states was significantly more represented in the signaling TCR-pMHC complex." — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39921257/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11993919/ ; publisher text: https://www.cell.com/biophysj/fulltext/S0006-3495(25)00059-1
- **Load-bearing interfaces and water access.** In load-dependent MD of TCRαβ–pMHC, contact layouts in some states "potentially make interfacial contacts more prone to break via easier access by water mol[ecules]" (snippet; full sentence truncated in the indexed text). — Chang-Gonzalez et al., bioRxiv 2023 (PMC10515854). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10515854

### 1.3 Solvation/desolvation in single-residue discrimination — the dedicated analyses

- **The dedicated biophysical analysis you are remembering is Wu et al., *J. Biol. Chem.* 298(3):101684 (2022), on TCR 6-11 vs p53 R175H–HLA-A2 — not the H2 TCRm.** Verbatim: "Structure-based in silico mutagenesis revealed that the 60-fold loss in 6-11 binding affinity for WT p53 compared to p53R175H is mainly due to the higher energetic cost of desolvating R175 in the WT p53 peptide during complex formation than H175 in the mutant. This indirect strategy for preferential neoantigen recognition by 6-11 is fundamentally different from the direct strategies employed by other TCRs…" Numbers: "The predicted ΔΔG value was 1.6 Rosetta energy units (REU; analogous to kcal/mol) (Table 3), consistent with the substantial (60-fold) loss…"; SPR: 6-11 binds p53R175H–HLA-A2 with KD = 3.5 ± 0.2 μM, WT with KD = 214 ± 19.8 μM ("∼60-fold weaker"); kinetics kon = 8.8 × 10^3 M−1 s−1, koff = 0.033 s−1. Rosetta term decomposition: "The terms correspond to attractive van der Waals (Eatr), repulsive van der Waals (Erep), desolvation (Esol), and hydrogen bonding (Ehbond) energies." — https://www.jbc.org/article/S0021-9258(22)00124-7/fulltext ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8897694 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35124005

- **Solvation dominates specificity in an affinity-matured melanoma TCR.** Madura et al. used phage display to raise a TCR (α24β17) "with a 30,000-fold enhanced binding affinity (KD = 0.6 nM)" and found: "although the enhanced affinity was mediated primarily through new TCR-MHC contacts, α24β17 remained acutely sensitive to modifications at every position along the peptide backbone, mimicking the specificity of the wild type TCR. Individual energetic components of the TCR-pHLA interaction governing affinity enhancements were [decomposed]…" — *Mol. Ther. Oncolytics* 18:443-456. **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32913893/
- **The KRAS G12D/JDI paper itself frames discrimination as solvation/thermodynamics with 'minimal changes in direct contacts'.** Poole et al., *Nat. Their isothermal titration calorimetry: "JDIa41b1 TCR interactions with both pHLAs were enthalpically driven, but with a greater favorable value for the JDIa41b1-HLA-A*11-KRAS G12D interaction (∆H = −20.3 kcal.mol–1) compared to JDIa41b1-HLA-A*11-KRAS WT (∆H = −9.6 kcal.mol–1)… Both TCR-pHLA complexes were entropically unfavorable, but with a greater unfavorable value for the JDIa41b1-HLA-A*11-KRAS G12D interaction (T∆S = −8.1 kcal.mol–1) compared to JDIa41b1-HLA-A*11-KRAS WT (T∆S = −1.8 kcal.mol–1)." ## 2. Peptide conformational dynamics in the groove

### 2.1 Conformational selection vs induced fit for pMHC recognition

- **Quantified across the PDB (apo vs holo): TCRs move, and mostly in the CDR3s.** McMaster et al. analysed apo/holo TCR:pMHC-I pairs: "Our analysis shows that all six CDR loops undergo conformational change between apo and holo states"; "We found that there were no cases where TCRs had larger than 1.0 Å backbone RMSD and smaller than 0.5 Å backbone RMSD between apo and holo conformations. This indicates that TCRs may operate in only one mode of protein-protein interaction." They frame the three modalities: "1) the 'lock-and-key' model… 2) the 'induced-fit' model… or 3) the 'pre-existing equilibrium' (also called 'conformational selection')…". They also summarise the field: "They state that these findings support the paradigm of induced fit occurring between TCRs and pMHC-Is. Other groups have focused on the conformational changes of pMHC-Is when engaged by TCRs. MD simulations show that the peptide and MHC molecule greatly affect each other's flexibility." — McMaster et al., *Front. Immunol.* 15:1491… (2024). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11646856 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39687625/
- **NMR evidence for 'induced-fit style' peptide behaviour in the groove.** Insaidoo, Zajicek & Baker labelled the Tax(11-19) peptide inside HLA-A*0201: "the data with the Tax(11-19) peptide indicate the presence of slow conformational exchange in the peptide, supporting an 'induced-fit' style TCR binding mechanism." — *Biochemistry* 48(41):9708-10 (2009). **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19772349/ ; PMC2762276
- **Kinetic data cannot distinguish the two models for one TCR — slow kon.** Gakamsky, Luescher & Pecht, *PNAS* (2004): "the time courses of TCR-MHCp interaction fit well to two different, yet closely related mechanisms, where an induced fit or a preequilibrium of two unb[ound states are invoked]"; association rate "decreasing from 7.0 x 10(3) at 25 degrees C to 1.8 x 10(2) M(-1).s(-1) at 4 degrees C". — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15178754/
- **Peptide length/bulge flexibility governs how much the TCR 'flattens' the peptide.** Liu et al. (HLA-B*3508, super-bulged 13-mer LPEP): "The flexible 11-mer determinant was flattened upon TCR ligation, enabling the TCR to make substantial contacts with HLA B*3501" (i.e. induced fit), whereas the rigid 13-mer remained bulged; the SB27 TCR response was "largely focused on a number of peptide-mediated contacts" with CDR2α αN50/αF52 H-bonding to HLA Glu154. — Liu et al., *J. Biol. Chem.* 287(15):12267 (2012). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3320977 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22343629/

### 2.2 The KRAS G12D decamer in HLA-A*11:01: register, bulge, and what the crystals show

- **In the TCR-free pMHC structure 7OW4, the mutated Asp6 is invisible — P5 (Ala) and P6 (Asp) have no density in any of the four copies.** **[computed]** From the deposited coordinates: `REMARK 465` lists "ALA C 5", "ASP C 6" (and the equivalents in chains F, I, L) as not located; the modelled peptide is only residues 1-4 and 7-10 (sequence VVVG…GVGK). Resolution 1.81 Å. Peptide mean B-factors per modelled residue: P1 49.2, P2 44.6, P3 41.7, P4 46.1, P7 47.0, P8 42.3, P9 39.7, P10 37.8 Å² (overall B 35.28 Å²). — https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW4.pdb ; https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW4 (primary citation: Poole et al., Nat. Commun. 13:5333, 2022)
- **In the JDI TCR complex 7OW6, all 10 peptide residues are ordered and Asp6 makes its buried salt bridge.** **[computed]** D6(OD2)–R114(NH2) = 2.95 Å; D6(OD1)–R114(NH1) = 3.04 Å; D6(OD1)–R114(NH2) = 3.34 Å; D6(OD2)–Q70(NE2) = 3.08 Å. Critically, **no Asp6 side-chain atom (CG/OD1/OD2) comes closer than 7.0 Å to any TCR atom** — the closest D6–TCR contact is the peptide backbone O of Asp6 to a TCR β-chain residue at 3.37 Å. I.e., the JDI TCR reads the backbone geometry at the mutation site, not the aspartate side chain itself. Resolution 2.64 Å; only 18 water molecules are modelled in the whole structure, so interfacial-water counts are not recoverable from this entry. — https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb ; https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW6
- **Consistent with the paper's own statements.** Poole et al.: "KRAS G12D peptide D6 side chain was buried in the HLA groove of the HLA-A*11-KRAS G12D-TCR complex"; "D6 in the KRAS G12D peptide was buried in the HLA groove and formed a salt bridge with R114 in the F-pocket of the HLA, which in turn was stabilized by HLA residue D116… The D6 side chain also made a hydrogen bond to HLA residue Q70… These interactions were not possible with the shorter side chain of G6 in the KRAS WT peptide." Section title: "KRAS G12D peptide conformation does not depend on affinity of bound TCR." Their MD found the H-bond occupancy of R28α–E58 "is low, especially in the JDIa41b1-HLA-A*11-KRAS G12D complex (<30%), consistent with the weak side-chain density observed." — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1
- **Two registers for G12D in A*11:01: 10-mer (productive, bulged) vs 9-mer (bent, non-productive).** Zhu et al., *Commun. Biol.* 9:26 (2025): "Notably, G12D peptides adopt two registers, a 9-mer and a 10-mer, that diverge sharply in structure and immunogenicity. The 10-mer forms a compact, stable pMHC with a TCR-accessible surface, while the 9-mer adopts a bent conformation incompatible with recognition. Molecular dynamics and NMR titration confirm the superior stability and binding affinity of the 10-mer." And: "Whereas C08:02 stabilizes both 9- and 10-mers via an Asp12 anchor at p3, our data indicate that in A11:01 the 10-mer shifts Asp12 to p6, enabling a stable bulged conformation, while the 9-mer remains conformationally flattened and poorly recognized. This explains why only the decamer forms a productive epitope for TCR engagement in the A11:01 context." **[computed] register check:** in the 9-mer structure 9UV8 the Asp is peptide residue 5 (VVGADGVGK), and its closest approach to HLA R114 is 7.1 Å (C–NH1) — i.e. no salt bridge in the 9-mer register, versus 2.95 Å in the 10-mer/JDI complex 7OW6. — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12774912 ; https://files.rcsb.org/download/9UV8.pdb ; https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9UV8
- **Solution NMR shows mutation-specific perturbations and a stabilising, compact decamer.** Zhu et al. reconstituted A*11:01 with WT and six G12 mutants (G12C/R/V/D/A/S): "Substantial chemical shift perturbations (CSPs) were detected in the HLA-A*11:01 heavy chain across the four mutant complexes, relative to the wild-type (WT) peptide"; the G12D side chain "inserts deeply into the groove, forming intramolecular contacts with HLA residues such as R114, thereby masking its identity and minimizing its exposure to the immune repertoire", while G12C/G12R side chains "extend outward from the MHC groove and remain solvent accessible". — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0

### 2.3 Mobility, breathing, and timescales

## 3. The peptide-free (empty) MHC-I

### 3.1 Classical observations

- **Empty MHC-I assembles but is heat-labile; low temperature rescues it.** Ljunggren et al., *Nature* 346:476 (1990): "culture of the murine lymphoma mutant cell line RMA-S at reduced temperature (19-33 degrees C) promotes assembly, and results in a high level of cell surface expression of H-2/beta 2-microglobulin complexes that do not present endogenous antigens, and are labile at 37 degrees C. They can be stabilized at 37 degrees C by exposure to specific peptides…" — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2198471/
- **Empty heterodimers can be native at 37 °C, and peptide binding buys only modest free energy.** Fahnestock et al., *Science* 258:1658 (1992): empty H-2Kd heterodimer renatured without peptide "was immunologically reactive and structurally similar to a heterodimer renatured in the presence of an appropriate restricted peptide. Thermal stability profiles indicated that the two forms of heterodimer differed in their resistance to denaturation by heat but that a significant portion of the empty class I heterodimers had a native conformation at physiological temperatures." — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1360705/ . Follow-up (Fahnestock et al., *Biochemistry* 33:??, 1994): "The majority of empty heterodimers are stable at 37 degrees C"; Kd values of four Kd-restricted peptides ranged "from 2.3 x 10(-7) to 3.4 x 10(-8) M". — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8025120/
- **On cells, peptide-receptive MHC-I is the short-lived population.** Su & Miller, *J. Immunol.* 167:4869 (2001): surface MHC-I splits into "two populations with mean half-life values of approximately 1 and 20 h"; peptide-receptive molecules "belong almost entirely to the less stable population"; "the maximum rate for binding of exogenous peptide corresponds to a t(1/2) value of 12 min"; and "approximately 20 short-lived molecules must be exported to the cell surface for each long-lived molecule". — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11673491/
- **'Empty' in cells is often really 'occupied by low-affinity peptides'.** De Silva et al., *J. Immunol.* 163:?? (1999): "at 26 degrees C Kb molecules in RMA-S cells are occupied with self-peptides. Such peptides stably associate with Kb at 26 degrees C but easily dissociate from them at 37 degrees C, suggesting low-affinity interactions." — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10510382/

### 3.2 Structural states of peptide-free MHC-I

- **Peptide-free HLA-A*02:01 X-ray structures: 'unlocked' pockets.** Anjanappa et al., *Nat. Commun.* 11:1314 (2020) — disulfide-stabilised HLA-A*02:01 (dsA2) "stable in the absence of peptide and can readily exchange cognate peptides"; "X-ray crystal structures of the peptide-free state of HLA-A*02:01, together with structures that have dipeptides bound in the A and F pockets. These structural snapshots reveal that the amino acid side chains lining the binding pockets switch in a coordinated fashion between a peptide-free unlocked state and a peptide-bound locked state." Mechanism detail: the Phe9–His70–Tyr99 triad re-arranges ("the A pocket has expanded… and thus appears ready to accommodate an incoming peptide") and the F pocket triad Arg97–His114–Tyr116 unlocks, with "a local concentration of positive charge at the center of the peptide-binding groove" in one state. Coordinates: 6TDR/6TDS (peptide-free), 6TDO/6TDP/6TDQ (dipeptides). — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7066147 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-14862-4

### 3.3 What a recombinant 'peptide-free HLA-A*11:01 + β2m' sample in an SPR/BLI assay is likely to be (synthesis of the above — inference, not a quoted finding)

- A metastable heterodimer in an **unlocked/partially collapsed groove state** (Anjanappa 2020; 2026 cryo-EM), heterogeneous across molecules (Zacharias & Springer 2004), with the peptide-binding domains locally unfolded (Kurimoto 2013).
- It **scavenges low-affinity peptides** from solution/serum components and from any residual peptide in the prep (De Silva 1999; Anjanappa 2020: "can readily exchange cognate peptides"), so a nominally empty sample is likely a mixture of truly empty + low-affinity-peptide-bound species.
- It is **heat-labile at 37 °C** (Ljunggren 1990) but a significant fraction can be native at physiological temperature (Fahnestock 1992/1994); on cells the receptive state has a ~1 h half-life with a 12 min maximum capture rate (Su & Miller 2001).
- For surface assays, immobilisation of this species risks capturing the collapsed/aggregated subpopulation; expect slow, heterogeneous binding and possible non-specific (hydrophobic) adhesion, and validate with a stabilised construct (disulfide-stabilised ± dipeptide, per Anjanappa 2020 / Saikia 2026) as the reference. No quantitative aggregation data for recombinant empty MHC-I was found in this search (see gaps).

## 4. HLA-A*11:01 groove specifics and physical context

### 4.1 Anchors and length preferences

- **Classical A*1101 motif (stabilisation assay on RMA-S transfectants, 136 peptides).** Chujoh et al., *Tissue Antigens* 52:501 (1998): "Lys at the C-terminus and Val, Ile, Phe, Tyr, and Thr at P2 are anchor residues for HLA-A*1101. Thr and aliphatic hydrophobic residues Val, Ile, and Leu at P2 are stronger anchor residues than the aromatic hydrophobic residues Phe and Tyr." Secondary anchors: "hydrophobic residues Leu, Phe, Tyr, Ile, and Ala at position 3 (P3)"; C-terminus can also be Arg. Length: "The affinities of the 8- and 12-mer peptides were significantly lower than those of 9- to 11-mer peptides. There was however no difference in affinity between 9-, 10- and 11-mer peptides." — **[abstract-only]** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9894848/
- **For the KRAS G12D epitope, the decamer is the productive species and the nonamer is not.** Zhu et al. 2025: "only the decamer forms a productive epitope for TCR engagement in the A11:01 context" (details in §2.2). — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0
- **Anchor chemistry of these KRAS peptides in A*11:01 (crystal).** Zhu et al.: "The backbone polar atoms of the three N-terminal valine residues formed robust hydrogen bonds with residues such as E87 in the A pocket, while the C-terminal lysine residue and the free carboxyl group established multiple interactions with D101, Y108, K170, and others in the F pocket… the primary anchoring regions for different KRAS G12 mutant peptides remain conserved… differences in TCR recognition are unlikely to involve the N- or C-terminal regions." — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0

### 4.2 Register and the P4–P6 bulge

- Decamer VVVGADGVGK: Asp at **p6**; "the 10-mer shifts Asp12 to p6, enabling a stable bulged conformation" (Zhu 2025). Nonamer VVGADGVGK: Asp at p5, "conformationally flattened and poorly recognized"; in 9UV8 no Asp–R114 salt bridge (**[computed]** closest D5–R114 atom pair 7.1 Å) versus 2.95 Å in the JDI/decamer complex 7OW6 (**[computed]**). — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0 ; https://files.rcsb.org/download/9UV8.pdb ; https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb

### 4.3 Allele-specific surface features relevant to binder placement

- **[computed] JDI TCR footprint on HLA-A*11:01 (7OW6):** 68 heavy-chain atoms within 4.5 Å of the TCR, spanning 16 residues, all on the two α-helices: α1 helix 58, 62, 65, 66, 68, 69, 72, 73, 75, 76 and α2 helix 150, 151, 154, 155, 158, 163. The peptide contacts of the parent/matured JDI TCR include CDR2α W53/W54 and CDR1α T30α (Poole: "CDR2α residue W53 (which directly contacted peptide residue D6), CDR2α residue Pro52 and CDR3β residue Lys94 played significant energetic roles"; "TCR CDR2α residues W53α and W54α that made hydrophobic contacts with HLAα2 residues Q155 and E154"). — https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1
- **The mutation itself is not on the binder-accessible surface.** **[computed]** Asp6 side chain ≥7.0 Å from any TCR atom in 7OW6 (backbone-mediated contact at 3.37 Å). This is why discrimination in this system must be read out through the surface: peptide backbone register/bulge shape, mutation-induced groove deformation, and electrostatics (Poole's Fig. 3: "Difference in binding energy is primarily driven by changes in electrostatic potential in pHLA mediated by the D6 mutation"). — https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1
- **A*11:01 prevalence:** "HLA-A*11:01 is highly prevalent in East Asian populations and has been shown to present multiple KRAS G12-derived epitopes" (Zhu 2025). — https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0

### 4.4 The N86 glycan relative to the groove and a binder footprint

- **[computed] Attachment-point geometry in the KRAS structures (no glycan atoms are modelled in 7OW4/7OW6/9UV8 — the only heteroatoms are ions/EDO/SO4-type):**
  - N86 ND2 → nearest peptide atom: **14.0 Å** (7OW4, nearest residue P10), **16.5 Å** (7OW6, nearest P10), **16.9 Å** (9UV8, nearest P9).
  - N86 ND2 → nearest JDI TCR atom in 7OW6: **18.6 Å**.
  - N86 ND2 → peptide N-terminus (P1): 35–37 Å in all three structures (the attachment point is at the C-terminal/F-pocket end of the α1 helix rim, not near the A pocket).
  - Only 1 water within 6 Å of N86 ND2 in 7OW4/9UV8; 0 in 7OW6.
  — https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW4.pdb , https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb , https://files.rcsb.org/download/9UV8.pdb
- **Interpretation:** the N86 attachment point sits at the groove rim, ~15 Å off the peptide axis and ~19 Å from the nearest TCR atom — outside the direct footprint of the JDI TCR — but a full high-mannose glycan (Glc3Man9GlcNAc2) is large enough to reach into the periphery of a binder footprint. No structure of an A*11:01 complex with the glycan modelled was found, and the KRAS pMHC reagents used for these structures are E. coli-refolded (Zhu: "The resulting pMHC-I complex was expressed in E. coli"), i.e. aglycosylated.
- **Glycan biology that matters for reagent design:** the N86 glycan is the quality-control handle for peptide loading — "MHC I heavy chains (hc) are co-translationally N-linked glycosylated at a conserved asparagine (Asn86) with a branched Glc3Man9GlcNAc2 glycan structure"; "peptide-receptive MHC I molecules are stabilized by multivalent chaperone interactions including the calreticulin-engulfed mono-glucosylated MHC I glycan, which only becomes accessible for processing by α-glucosidase II upon loading of optimal epitopes" (PLC cryo-EM at 3.7 Å). — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9365787 ; and: "Peptide-loaded MHC-I complexes then dissociate from the PLC once glucosidase II has removed the last glucose from the N-linked glycan attached to asparagine 86 (N86) of MHC-I" — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913765
- **Glycan effects on TCR kinetics (MD):** "We find that weak glycan-protein or glycan-glycan interactions impact the equilibrated structure of the TCR and pMHC leading to an increase in the overall bond strength of the TCR-pMHC complex" — MD study of N-glycosylation effects on TCR kinetics (UCDavis George lab PDF). https://georgelab.bme.ucdavis.edu/files/2022/10/A-molecular-dynamics-investigation-of-N-glycosylation.pdf

## Physical/chemical factors checklist for our design (each bullet grounded in a cited finding)

1. **The mutation is buried; you cannot design a direct Asp6 side-chain contact in the JDI binding mode.** In 7OW6 the Asp6 side chain stays ≥7.0 Å from the TCR (closest D6–TCR contact is backbone O at 3.37 Å), and in the TCR-free 7OW4 the whole P5–P6 segment is disordered. [https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW6.pdb ; https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW4.pdb ; Poole 2022 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1]
2. **Discriminate through the readouts that the mutation actually changes: backbone/bulge geometry, groove deformation, electrostatics, and desolvation.** "Difference in binding energy is primarily driven by changes in electrostatic potential in pHLA mediated by the D6 mutation"; ΔH is 10.7 kcal/mol more favourable for G12D than WT in the matured JDI (ΔH −20.3 vs −9.6 kcal/mol), offset by a larger entropic penalty (TΔS −8.1 vs −1.8 kcal/mol). [Poole 2022 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1]
3. **Design against the decamer register (Asp at p6, bulged P4–P6); do not target the 9-mer register.** The 9-mer "remains conformationally flattened and poorly recognized"; only the decamer is productive in A*11:01; the nonamer's Asp does not even make the R114 salt bridge (7.1 Å vs 2.95 Å). [Zhu 2025 https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0 ; computed from 9UV8/7OW6]
4. **Expect conformational selection/induced fit and budget for entropy.** The central bulge is mobile/disordered without a binder (7OW4 P5–P6 missing; 9UV8 central B-factors 26.6–27.9 vs 14–15 Å² at the termini); TCR binding rigidifies the peptide at an entropic cost that was measured at TΔS = −8.1 kcal/mol for JDIa41b1/G12D. Design either to stabilise the preferred bulge or to exploit a rigid pre-formed segment. [https://files.rcsb.org/download/7OW4.pdb ; Poole 2022 ; Insaidoo 2009 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19772349/ ; McMaster 2024 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11646856]
5. **Treat interfacial water as part of the design surface, not noise.** The groove contains a conserved water network (63 conserved clusters; 14 long-lived MD waters) that "enhance[s] affinity without contributing to epitope specificity"; interfacial waters "form bridging hydrogen bonds that enhance polar interactions and neutralize unpaired hydrogen-bonding groups"; and water's contribution differs between TCRs (enhancing D3, weakening 2B4). Score designs with explicit desolvation terms (Rosetta Esol-style ΔΔG, as in Wu 2022, where desolvation was the dominant term). [Ogata & Wodak 2002 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12364585/ ; Petrone & Garcia 2004 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15066441/ ; Anikeeva 2003 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12705834/ ; Wu 2022 https://www.jbc.org/article/S0021-9258(22)00124-7/fulltext]
6. **Target a discrimination margin that matches what physics delivers: single-digit kcal/mol.** Measured discrimination windows: 6-11 TCR 60-fold (ΔΔG 1.6 REU; KD 3.5 vs 214 µM); JDI parent 63 µM with no measurable WT binding, matured to >4000–6000-fold (KD down to 50–100 pM, t1/2 ≥20 h); H2 86 nM with direct contacts. A design with no measurable WT binding at the assay's detection limit is the realistic goal, not a large computed ΔΔG. [Wu 2022 ; Poole 2022 (Table 2) ; Frontiers 2023 Table 2 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2023.1303304/full]
7. **Exploit electrostatic complementarity to the mutation-induced potential shift, but expect to pay for it in entropy.** JDI's gain comes with "a larger net increase in new interactions" and higher rigidity (TΔS −8.1 kcal/mol). Positive-potential CDR/binder loops over the P5–P7 zone are the natural motif. [Poole 2022 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1]
8. **For the peptide-free MHC selectivity arm: assume a metastable, unlocked, partially collapsed target that scavenges peptides.** Peptide-free MHC-I is "conformationally significantly more heterogeneous… within the alpha-helices that flank the peptide binding cleft"; the groove partially collapses during exchange; the peptide-binding domains are unstructured in the peptide-free NMR form; and RMA-S 'empty' molecules are in fact occupied by low-affinity peptides. Build assays so that non-specific binding to collapsed/aggregated material is distinguishable (e.g., use disulfide-stabilised ± dipeptide-stabilised empty MHC as reference; block with excess β2m/dipeptide controls). [Zacharias & Springer 2004 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15454423/ ; 2026 cryo-EM https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13562589/ ; Kurimoto 2013 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23578712/ ; De Silva 1999 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10510382/ ; Anjanappa 2020 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7066147 ; Saikia 2026 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41479000/]
9. **Mind the temperature and the surface.** Empty MHC-I is "labile at 37 degrees C" but "a significant portion… had a native conformation at physiological temperatures"; on cells the peptide-receptive state is the ~1 h half-life population with a 12 min maximum capture rate. SPR/BLI immobilisation at 25 °C vs 37 °C will sample different states of the empty target. [Ljunggren 1990 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2198471/ ; Fahnestock 1992 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1360705/ ; Su & Miller 2001 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11673491/]
10. **Binder placement: the open surface is the two α-helices; the glycan attachment is off to the C-terminal rim.** JDI contacts 16 helix residues (α1 58–76; α2 150–163) and reads the peptide backbone over P5–P7; N86 ND2 sits 18.6 Å from the nearest JDI atom (and the KRAS pMHC reagents are E. coli-refolded/aglycosylated). A binder centred over the P4–P7 zone with a footprint similar to JDI's is physically compatible with the groove rim and glycan-free material. [computed from 7OW6 ; Zhu 2025 (E. coli refolding) ; glycan biology: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9365787]
11. **Do not rely on N-/C-terminal anchor differences (there are none between G12D and WT) and do not design to the 8-mer or 12-mer forms.** Anchors (P2 Val/Thr/Ile, C-term Lys) and N/C-terminal H-bond networks are identical between mutant and WT; 8- and 12-mers bind A*1101 significantly worse than 9–11-mers, and 9/10/11-mers are equivalent in affinity — the decamer is the species of record for this epitope. [Chujoh 1998 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9894848/ ; Zhu 2025 https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-09285-0]
12. **If functional (cell-based) selectivity matters, remember the interface is mechanically load-bearing.** TCR–pMHC discrimination can be load- and force-dependent (catch-bond regime 10–20 pN; contact layouts alter water access and breakage). An affinity-only SPR win may not translate to a signalling-competent binder. [Chang-Gonzalez 2023 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10515854 ; Golovin 2025 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39921257/]

## Gaps and unknowns

- **No peptide-free HLA-A*11:01 structure or stability measurement was found.** Peptide-free structural data exist for HLA-A*02:01 (X-ray; Anjanappa 2020) and HLA-Cw*07:02 (NMR; Kurimoto 2013). Anything allele-specific for A*11:01's empty state is an extrapolation.
- **No water quantification at a KRAS G12D/A*11:01 interface exists.** 7OW6 (2.64 Å) models only 18 waters; the quantitative water studies (Petrone & Garcia; Ogata & Wodak) are on MHC–peptide, not TCR–pMHC; a dedicated MD study of the JDI interface waters was not found. Could not verify.
- **No published free-energy (FEP/MM-PBSA/umbrella-sampling) comparison of G12D vs WT for any A*11:01-restricted TCR** was found; the mechanistic evidence is structural + ITC + Rosetta ΔΔG analogues (Poole 2022; Wu 2022 for p53). Zhu 2025 includes MD but the paper text accessible here reports qualitative conclusions (stability/affinity of the decamer) rather than free-energy numbers.
- **The 'H2 desolvation' premise is corrected, not confirmed:** H2 discriminates p53 R175H by direct contacts (86 nM; "Direct antibody contacts with mutation"); the desolvation-based discrimination paper is Wu et al. 2022 on TCR 6-11 (60-fold, ΔΔG 1.6 REU). If a different H2-related biophysical paper exists, it was not found (searched).
- **'Zacharias & Springer 1992' does not appear to exist**; the matching work is Zacharias & Springer, *Biophys. J.* 87:2203 (2004).
- **Clone name 'KT5' could not be verified** in any KRAS G12D/HLA-A*11:01 paper found; verified names: JDI (7OW6/7PB2), TCR1/TCR1a7, KDA11-01/02, KT18 (G12V).
- **Aggregation/precipitation numbers for recombinant empty MHC-I** (e.g., % aggregate by SEC after X h at 37 °C) were not found in this pass; the "aggregation-prone" expectation for the empty-MHC selectivity arm is inference from the instability literature above, not a quoted measurement. Treat as unverified.
- **Whether the N86 glycan sterically clashes with a binder footprint on A*11:01** was not found in the literature; the geometric statement here (18.6 Å attachment-point distance) is my computation from 7OW6, and no glycan-modelled A*11:01 complex was located to test it directly. Also note that the competition target's glycosylation state depends on its expression system (unknown to us) — E. coli-refolded material is aglycosylated; mammalian-expressed material will carry the glycan.
- **Peptide-exchange intermediates are now cryo-EM-accessible (2026) but A*11:01-specific exchange dynamics remain unpublished**; also, the 2026 cryo-EM work is on tapasin-dependent MHC-I, which may not map directly onto the dipeptide-stabilised recombinant material used in binding assays.
