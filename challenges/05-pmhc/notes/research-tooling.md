**Target:** KRAS G12D peptide `VVVGADGVGK` on HLA-A*11:01 (3-chain pMHC; binder = 4th chain). Anthropic × Adaptyv 2026, Challenge 5 (open 2026-10-26 → 2026-11-08).
**Local stack assumed:** RFdiffusion3 + Boltz-2 (+ipSAE) + ProteinMPNN on one 12 GB RTX 5070 (WSL2). Local clones inspected read-only: `~/tools/{binder-prescreen,BindCraft2,IPSAE,RFD4-Proteina,ProteinMPNN}`.
**Method note:** every claim below is URL-backed; local-clone evidence is given by path. "Could not verify" is used where a fetch failed or no source was found. Nothing was installed or modified. ### 0.3 `adaptyvbio/competition_metrics` — the official in-silico ranking
Repo: https://github.com/adaptyvbio/competition_metrics (Apache-2.0; README says "round 2", created 2024-10 — whether the 2026 challenge reuses it is **not verified**).
Rank-average of three metrics: `s = ⅓ [ r↑(iPTM) + r↓(PAE-interaction) + r↑(ESM-PLL) ]`, fractional ranking for ties.
- iPTM + PAE-interaction: ColabFold AF2 **with templates** (inspired by `dl_binder_design`), `colabfold_batch input.fasta out --num-recycle 3 --num-seeds 3 --num-models 5 --templates`; binder must come **first** in the FASTA. Script `compute_af2_metrics.py` takes `--target_length` (default 621 = EGFR — for the pMHC use ~390; it computes `pae_interaction` as the mean of the two off-diagonal PAE blocks).
- ESM-PLL: pseudolikelihood of `esm2_t33_650M_UR50D` on the binder sequence only (`compute_pll.py`).
- `rank.py` computes the rank average from a CSV (`--asc esm_pll --asc iptm --desc pae_interaction`).

## 2. Folding/scoring feasibility on a 12 GB card (WSL2)

Complex size to fold: HLA heavy chain 281 aa + b2M 99 aa + peptide 10 aa + binder ~100 aa ≈ **490 tokens** (3 states per design: G12D, WT, peptide-free).

### Boltz-2
- Reported limits: **~1600 tokens on 24 GB** consumer GPUs (Boltz-2 baseline); the **LMI4Boltz** fork raises this to >2356 tokens (tensor offload + in-place ops, "near-exact" outputs) and >2660 tokens with aggressive chunking (+8.5 % wall time at 1596 tokens): https://github.com/tlitfin/lmi4boltz , https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.29.684571 .
- ChimeraX guidance (Tom Litfin / RBVI): on Linux, 8–12 GB GPUs handle only **~300–500 residues** before OOM (no CPU fallback on Linux; Windows falls back to CPU at 10–20× slowdown). A 12 GB RTX 4070 example predicted up to ~1000 residues *on Windows*. With their fork's `--use_cpu_memory` and `--aggressive_chunking` flags, consumer-GPU limits rise further (commit: https://github.com/RBVI/boltz/commit/a70911c4fcadb3264d69f6534e40e4d56476ad55 ).
- Issue data points: 8 GB P4000 ran a ~370-residue affinity example at ~4.2 GB but crashed a ~320-residue one at >7 GB (https://github.com/jwohlwend/boltz/issues/575 ).
- **Assessment:** ~490 tokens is borderline on 12 GB Linux with stock Boltz-2 — likely fine for structure-only with reduced MSA and 1 sample, tight for the affinity module. Levers (all in `boltz predict`): `--max_msa_seqs` (default 8192 → set 1024), `--subsample_msa --num_subsampled_msa 1024`, `--max_parallel_samples 1`, `--no_kernels`, `--use_cpu_memory` + `--aggressive_chunking` (RBVI/LMI4Boltz forks), or run LMI4Boltz directly (drop-in). Optionally `msa: empty` (single-sequence) for the binder chain only. Docs: https://github.com/jwohlwend/boltz/blob/main/docs/prediction.md . Pocket constraint syntax for guided screening is in the same doc: `constraints: [{pocket: {binder: D, contacts: [C, 6], max_distance: 6.0}}]`.
- WSL2 caveat: the Windows CPU-fallback behavior does **not** apply (WSL2 is Linux) — plan on the LMI4Boltz/RBVI fork for headroom.

### Chai-1
- Hard limit: **2048 tokens**; maintainers' own guidance in issue #153: A100 40 GB ≈ 1500 tokens, A100 80 GB ≈ 2048; a README update notes **L40S 48 GB is enough for 2048 AA**. 10 GB cards OOM at defaults (issue #17); 4090 users report success; README recommends A100/H100 80 GB but A10/A30 "work for smaller complexes": https://github.com/chaidiscovery/chai-lab/issues/153 , https://github.com/chaidiscovery/chai-lab/issues/17 , https://github.com/chaidiscovery/chai-lab/releases .
- Chai-1 has a **low-memory regime** (PR #172, release v0.5.2 "lower memory consumption by default… low-memory regime"), and MSA-free mode is the default (embeddings only) — both help on 12 GB.
- **Assessment:** ~490 tokens is far below the documented limits; 12 GB is not officially listed but plausibly sufficient, especially MSA-free. Cross-check with restraints: CSV format `chainA, res_idxA, chainB, res_idxB, connection_type, confidence, min_distance_angstrom, max_distance_angstrom` — e.g. a `pocket` restraint between binder chain and peptide residue 6 (`res_idx` format `D6`/`G6`, validated against the sequence; `chainA` empty for the binder side of a pocket restraint). Docs: https://github.com/chaidiscovery/chai-lab/blob/main/examples/restraints/README.md . Chains are lettered A–Z in input order.

### If local folding fails
AF3 Server (https://alphafoldserver.com — quota'd, used by a public KRAS campaign for on/off-target pairs) and Protenix (https://github.com/bytedance/Protenix) are the main alternatives; ColabFold/AF2 is already in the stack (and is what the organizers' metrics use).

## 3. (b) Concrete in-silico specificity-screening recipes found in public code

### Recipe 1 — Three-state differential folding with a positive-control gate (public competition repo, Challenge 5)
Source: https://github.com/rpinho/adaptyv-challenges (`c5_prep/`, docs dated 2026-10-27). This repo is *literally* preparing Challenge 5 (KRAS G12D pMHC).
1. Define three states: **G12D complex**, **WT complex** (`VVVGAGGVGK`), **peptide-free** HLA+b2m (plus an optional HLA-only/other-peptide panel).
2. **Positive control first:** fold the JDI TCR from 7OW6 (chains D+E) against all three states. Published SPR for JDI: KD 63 µM vs G12D; no binding vs WT, G12C, ERAS, RRAS, MRAS, DIRAS2, RAP1A, self-peptide pool (Poole et al., Nat Commun 2022, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-32811-1).
3. **Their measured verdict (important):** ColabFold AF2-Multimer ipTM gave **0.84 (G12D) = 0.84 (WT)** at best-model level → *the pipeline cannot discriminate the known binder from the known non-binder*. Mean-ipTM ordering was right (0.818 / 0.772 / 0.710) but driven by one WT outlier — "fragile and unjustified". Peptide-present vs peptide-free *is* discriminable (Δ≈0.07). File: `c5_prep/C5_CONTROL_VALIDATION_RESULTS_20261027.md`.
4. Their proposed fixes: physics rescoring (FoldX ΔΔG on the D→G mutation; Rosetta interface ΔΔG with repack/minimization; **APBS electrostatic difference map** between G12D and WT states — CPU, $0), sequential gates instead of a weighted composite, and designing the discrimination mechanism in (direct Asp contacts or charge complementarity above the groove). Files: `C5_SCORING_STRATEGY_PANEL_20261027.md`, `C5_APBS_ELECTROSTATIC_DIFFERENCE_20261027.md`, `C5_COMPUTE_NOTES_20261027.md`.
5. Their structural finding (measured, worth re-checking on 7OW6 yourself): Asp6 side chain is ~90 % buried in the groove (SASA 8.8 Å²; OD2 0.00 Å²), salt-bridged to HLA Arg114 — the TCR's nearest atom to the Asp carboxylate is 7.5 Å; TCR contacts at P6 are backbone-only. Implication: "design direct H-bonds to the Asp" may be geometrically unavailable; the practical discrimination is charge/electrostatics above the P6–P8 bulge.

### Recipe 2 — Contact-level + MPNN specificity filters (public KRAS campaign repo)
Source: https://github.com/ElainW/agent_pmhc_kras_binder_2 (scripts are numbered and auditable; target prepped from PDB 9UV8, HLA-A*11:01).
- **Cheap geometric pre-filter before MPNN/AF2** (`scripts/05_contact_filter.py`): PyRosetta `ContactMolecularSurface` of binder vs **peptide residues 181–189** and vs the **mutant residue alone** (Asp at B185 in their 9-mer numbering) — ~1–2 s/design vs ~200 s for AF2. Only backbones that reach the peptide (and the mutant position) proceed.
- **Hotspot choice** (`scripts/02_run_rfdiffusion.sh`): hotspots **B184–186** (peptide P4–P6), centered on the Asp.
- **MPNN-based specificity without folding** (`scripts/08_mpnn_specificity.py`): ProteinMPNN `conditional_probs_only` conditioned on the bound binder gives the full 21-AA distribution at the mutation position; score = `log_p(on-target aa) − log_p(Ala)`. Primary, always-available specificity gate.
- **AF3-server paired jobs** (`scripts/prepare_af3_jobs.py`): one on-target (peptide `VVGADGVGK`) and one off-target (peptide `VVGAGGVGK`, PDB 8I5E) fold per candidate, batched to the AF3 Server quota.
- **Caveat for this competition:** they used a **9-mer** peptide (D at peptide position 5 of their numbering, B185); the competition peptide is the **10-mer `VVVGADGVGK`** (D at position 6 → e.g. B186 in a 1–180+peptide numbering). Do not copy their residue numbers blindly.

### Recipe 3 — The published pMHC-binder filter pipeline (Science 2025 code)
Source: https://github.com/77forest/pMHCI_binder_design (Liu et al., Science 2025, https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adv0185 ; code also at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15169815 — Zenodo fetch was rate-limited, GitHub mirror read instead).
- **pMHC prep convention:** trim to the relevant HLA residues (~first 180), **renumber continuously from 1 and relabel MHC + peptide as one chain B** (so the binder designs against a 2-chain system: binder + merged pMHC). Their scaffolds folder + `align_chainB.py` dock prior binder scaffolds onto a new pMHC; then **partial diffusion** to adapt them (the paper's "privileged scaffold" route — the same route produced binders for gp100/MART-1/PRAME from one scaffold).
- **Contact filter** (`contact_filter/contact_residue_filter.py` + Rosetta `software/contact/just_contact_patch.xml`): computes Rosetta `contact_patch` **per peptide residue (181–189)** and filters with `awk '{if ($1>0 && … && $8>0) print}'` — i.e. require contact to **every** peptide position; this is the operational version of "extensive contacts with the peptide".
- **MPNN peptide-scan specificity** (`mpnn_spec_filter/mpnn_spec_scores.py`): run a ProteinMPNN "pepscan" (zero-shot predictions: WT vs each single mutant of the peptide in the context of the designed binder) and keep designs where `log_p_mt − log_p_wt < −0.5` across all peptide positions (the binder "expects" the on-target residue over alanine) — this is the paper's Fig. S1C specificity filter.
- Paper-level screening (from the Science text): ProteinMPNN peptide-sequence confidence on-target vs off-target, then a **fine-tuned AF2 model** (Motmaen et al., https://github.com/phbradley/alphafold_finetune ; HLA-retrained variant https://github.com/titaniumsg/afft_hla3db ) used to score each design against the target peptide and closely related proteome peptides — select designs where the on-target complex is confidently predicted and off-targets are not.

### Recipe 4 — Mapping to your stack (Boltz-2 + ipSAE + MPNN), per design
1. **Fold 3–4 states** with Boltz-2, 4 chains each (A=HLA 281, B=b2M 99, C=peptide, D=binder): G12D peptide; WT peptide; peptide-free (A+B+D only; a "no peptide" YAML); optionally a control on-target with the JDI TCR replacing the binder.
2. **Score per state with ipSAE** (`ipsae.py <pae.npz> <cif> 10 10`; Boltz-2 supported in the local clone, changelog 2026-01-03): read the **binder–peptide (D–C)** row plus binder–HLA (D–A) and binder–b2M (D–B). Organizers' thresholds: ipSAE_min > 0.61 standard / > 0.70 stringent; pDockQ > 0.5.
3. **Specificity margins (not raw scores):** ΔipSAE(D–C) = ipSAE(D–C | G12D) − ipSAE(D–C | WT) and ΔpDockQ2; require binder–HLA/b2M rows to stay weak (avoids the "general MHC binder" failure mode). The agentic pipeline https://github.com/92kunheekim/pmhc-agent formalises this as a worst-case margin over an off-target panel: `accept ⇔ min_over_offtargets [score(on) − score(off)] ≥ θ` plus a `peptide_energy_fraction ≥ φ` gate (README "specificity contract"); use `min`, not mean.
4. **Contact-level checks at peptide position 6** (adapting Recipe 2): count H-bonds/salt bridges from binder atoms to Asp6 OD1/OD2 in the G12D model (Rosetta/PyRosetta or a simple distance/H-bond script), require the same interactions to be absent in the WT model (Gly6 has no side chain), and measure how much of peptide P6 SASA the binder buries (compare peptide SASA in pMHC alone vs in the complex — the rpinho/Fable-style measurement). A cheap pre-filter for backbones: `ContactMolecularSurface` vs the peptide and vs residue 6 alone.
5. **MPNN specificity cross-check:** `conditional_probs_only` peptide scan (ElainW `08_mpnn_specificity.py`; 77forest `mpnn_spec_filter`) — `log_p(on-target) − log_p(Ala) > 0` at P6 and the alanine-scan filter `log_p_mt − log_p_wt < −0.5` at all peptide positions.
6. **Physics tie-breaker (optional, bounded):** FoldX ΔΔG(G12D→WT) or Rosetta interface ΔΔG with repack+minimization; APBS difference map as a mechanism check (Recipe 1). The public panel's consensus: give physics one day on the JDI control; if it can't rank the control, don't use it as a filter.
7. **Gate order suggested by the public material:** viability (pLDDT/fold) → interface quality (ipSAE/PAE) → mutation-dependence (P6 contacts + Δmargins) → negative design vs WT & HLA-only → diversity of the final 20.

### Recipe 5 — Chai-1 cross-check with pocket restraint
Fold binder+D against A,B,C with a pocket restraint on peptide position 6 (CSV: `restraint0,D,,C,D6,pocket,1.0,0.0,6.0` style; see https://github.com/chaidiscovery/chai-lab/blob/main/examples/restraints/README.md ) — the restraint guides the interface; specificity still comes from steps 2–6. Chai restraints measurably improve interface DockQ when contacts are supplied.

## 5. (c) Gaps / unknowns

1. **No wet-lab-validated de novo binder against KRAS G12D/HLA-A*11:01 is public** (as of 2026-10-30). The prescreen's `kras-pmhc` set is TCR-only and states no TCR-mimic/designed-binder sequences exist in its sources. The JDI TCR (7OW6) + IMC-KRASG12D is the natural positive control.
2. **Single-mutation discrimination in silico is unproven for this system.** The only public measurement found (rpinho, 2026-10-27) shows AF2-Multimer ipTM cannot separate the JDI TCR's G12D vs WT complexes (0.84 = 0.84), and the paper's own mechanism (Poole et al. 2022) is indirect electrostatics. Whether Boltz-2/ipSAE/Chai-1 fare better is untested — run the JDI control through your own pipeline before trusting any Δ-score.
3. **Asp6 is largely buried** (measured SASA 8.8 Å²; OD2 0.00 Å²; salt-bridged to HLA R114) per the public C5 analysis — a direct-H-bond-to-Asp design strategy may be geometrically unavailable; I did not independently re-measure this. Treat "hotspots on the Asp side chain" with caution.
4. **No official RFdiffusion3 pMHC example.** RFD3 pMHC configs found are third-party (BinderGeneration, pepbench) and unbenchmarked; RFD3's `select_hotspots` atom-level conditioning on a peptide (e.g. backbone + Asp tip atoms) is documented generally but not demonstrated on a 3-chain pMHC.
5. **RFdiffusion2 is not a path here** — no documented protein-binder/hotspot workflow (motif/enzyme/ligand tasks only).
6. **BindCraft pMHC evidence is weak**: official multi-chain support is documented (wiki + issue #169), but the only pMHC recipe found is a LinkedIn post; no repo/paper benchmark. BindCraft2's `bindcraft score` (hotspot/off-epitope contact fractions, coldspots) is usable as an extra QC layer regardless.
7. **"dMHC" as a design tool could not be verified** — only the delta-MHC assay and Immundex dextramers exist under that name.
8. **EvoBind2 is the wrong direction** (peptide-as-binder).
9. **Memory numbers at exactly this 4-chain size are unpublished.** Boltz-2: 24 GB ≈ 1600 tokens baseline; 12 GB Linux ≈ 300–500 residues before OOM (ChimeraX), borderline for a ~490-token complex without LMI4Boltz/RBVI optimizations or MSA capping. Chai-1: 2048-token cap, 12 GB not officially listed, low-memory regime exists. No pMHC-specific memory report found for either.
10. **Fetch failures:** Zenodo 15169815 (429); RFdiffusion3 standalone repo (does not exist — it is `RosettaCommons/foundry`, branch `production`; `main` 404s). Foundry issue #192 ("hotspots choosing": residue- vs atom-level, how many, which criteria) is **open and unanswered** as of this research — the hotspot-design question for RFD3 is still community-open.
11. **Competition scoring for Challenge 5 is not verified.** `competition_metrics` documents a round-2 (2024) rank-average of iPTM/ipAE/ESM-PLL; whether the 2026 pMHC challenge reuses it (and whether WT/peptide-free counter-screens enter the in-silico score) should be confirmed on the challenge page (https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026). Note the organizers' own metric set contains no mutation-specificity term — specificity is an experimental readout (on-target vs WT vs peptide-free binding), so in-silico specificity screening is a design-quality lever, not a leaderboard one.
