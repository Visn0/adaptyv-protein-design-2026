# Mechanism and prior art: mutation-specific pMHC binders for KRAS G12D on HLA-A*11:01

Prepared 2026-10-30 (CET) for the Anthropic x Adaptyv 2026 protein design competition, Challenge 05 (KRAS G12D pMHC, https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026/challenges/pmhc). Scope: (1) how TCRs discriminate KRAS G12D from wild-type (WT) peptide on HLA-A*11:01; (2) TCR-mimic (TCRm) antibodies and biologics against pMHC neoantigens; (3) de novo computational design of pMHC binders 2023-2026 including failures/limitations; (4) target biology; (5) assay and quantitative expectations. Every item carries a source URL. Method: Europe PMC REST (exact citations and abstracts), RCSB PDB API plus direct analysis of deposited coordinates (7OW3/7OW4/7OW5/7OW6/7PB2), bioRxiv API, and direct fetches of open-access full texts. Distances marked [computed] were measured by me from the deposited coordinates (atom-atom minimum distances); everything else is quoted from the cited source. Inaccessible sources are flagged in Gaps.

## 1. TCR discrimination of KRAS G12D vs WT on HLA-A*11:01

Key paper: Poole A, Karuppiah V, Hartt A, et al. Therapeutic high affinity T cell receptor targeting a KRASG12D cancer neoantigen. Nat Commun 2022;13:5333. DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-32811-1. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32811-1 | PMID 36088370 | full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9464187/ | PDB entries released 2022-07-20.

Structures (RCSB): 7OW3 apo HLA-A*11:01 + WT peptide VVVGAGGVGK https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW3 ; 7OW4 apo HLA-A*11:01 + G12D peptide VVVGADGVGK https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW4 ; 7OW5 affinity-enhanced JDIa41b1 TCR + WT complex (2.64 A) https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW5 ; 7OW6 JDIa41b1 TCR + G12D complex (2.58 A) https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7OW6 ; 7PB2 parent JDI TCR + G12D https://www.rcsb.org/structure/7PB2 .

Parent TCR (JDI), SPR data (Table 1 of the paper, PMC9464187):
- KD = 63 uM for HLA-A*11:01-KRAS G12D; no measurable binding (NB) to KRAS WT, G12C, ERAS (VVVGASGVGK), RRAS, MRAS, DIRAS2, RAP1A peptides, or pools of A*11-presented self-peptides. The unengineered TCR is thus already fully G12D-specific; the mutation is read without any engineered contacts.
- Affinity maturation (Table 2): JDIa9bwt (1st gen) 261 nM vs G12D / 25.8 uM vs WT (98-fold window); JDIa41b1 (2nd gen) 743 pM vs G12D / 3.0 uM vs WT (>4000-fold window); JDIa96b35 (3rd gen) 63.7 pM vs G12D / 425 nM vs WT (>6000-fold window, t1/2 >20 h). Several variants reached 50-100 pM with t1/2 >= 20 h; overall over one million-fold affinity increase over the parent TCR (abstract), with discrimination retained or improved.
- Therapeutic format: JDIa96b35 TCR fused to a humanized anti-CD3 scFv (IMC-KRASG12D ImmTAC) selectively kills G12D-expressing cancer cells.

Mechanism of discrimination (the central result):
- Crystal structures of JDIa41b1 bound to WT vs G12D are virtually identical (TCR variable-domain RMSD 0.29 A; identical 50 degree crossing angle; the TCR engages mainly the peptide C-terminus via CDR3beta). Specificity is not from direct TCR-mutant contacts: thermodynamic analysis gives dH = -20.3 kcal/mol (G12D) vs -9.6 kcal/mol (WT) for the same TCR, and molecular dynamics shows the buried Asp6 shifts the HLA surface electrostatics, increasing electrostatic interactions with the TCR (specificity via indirect electrostatic effects).
- How the mutant Asp at peptide position 6 is read: in the unbound pHLA structures (7OW3, 7OW4) both peptides adopt a central bulge at residues 4-6 with P6 exposed/upward-facing; upon TCR binding, P6 flips down and becomes buried. In the G12D complex the buried Asp6 forms a bidentate salt bridge with HLA Arg114 in the F-pocket (Asp6 OD2 to Arg114 NH2 = 2.95 A; Asp6 OD1 to Arg114 NH1 = 3.04 A [computed from 7OW6]) and hydrogen bonds with HLA Gln70 (Asp6 OD2 to Gln70 NE2 = 3.08 A; Asp6 N to Gln70 OE1 = 3.02 A [computed]), with Arg114 stabilized by HLA Asp116 (paper text; no direct Asp6-Asp116 contact below 4.5 A [computed] - the stabilization is indirect, through Arg114). In the WT complex Gly6 makes none of these contacts (closest approach to Arg114 6.6 A, to Gln70 4.7 A [computed from 7OW5]). So the mutant is read as a composite epitope: Asp6 clamped against the HLA groove, changing both local chemistry and surface electrostatics.
- Peptide mobility at the center: in the apo G12D structure 7OW4, peptide positions 5-6 are not modeled (disordered). Verified from deposited coordinates: peptide chain C has residues 1-4 and 7-10 only. The center of this 10-mer is intrinsically mobile - the same region the TCR exploits.

Cross-reactivity data for the JDI TCR (same paper):
- An A*11 single-chain HLA (scHLA) phage library of 1e10 random 10-mers was panned against the high-affinity TCR; the 5 most enriched peptides (96% of sequenced clones) all share the GADG motif at P4-P7. Ten human-proteome mimetic candidates were derived from the specificity profile; only 2 bound at all: RASL10A (460 nM) and DNHD1 (2.3 uM), both weak relative to the pM-range on-target affinity; DNHD1 produced about 12% of the G12D response in an ELISPOT.
- The paper also notes TCR cross-reactivity generally (example: a MAGE-A3 TCR cross-reacts with a Titin peptide).

Related A*11:01/G12D prior art:
- Wang QJ, et al. Identification of T-cell Receptors Targeting KRAS-Mutated Human Tumors. Cancer Immunol Res 2016. DOI 10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-15-0188. https://doi.org/10.1158/2326-6066.CIR-15-0188 | PMID 26701267. HLA-A*11:01-transgenic mice were immunized; murine TCRs highly reactive to KRAS G12V and G12D were isolated. Same paper: G12D and G12V are present in 60-70% of pancreatic and 20-30% of colorectal cancers.
- Contrast with the other clinically validated G12D allele (HLA-C*08:02): there the mutant Asp6 acts as an ANCHOR - it forms a salt bridge to HLA-C*08:02 Arg156, and only the mutant (not WT) peptide stabilizes HLA-C*08:02 (Sim MJW, et al. PNAS 2020;117:12826-12835. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1921964117. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1921964117 | PMID 32461371; TCR affinities nanomolar to low micromolar). On A*11:01, by contrast, both WT and mutant peptides bind well (structures 7OW3/7OW4; 7OW5/7OW6), so discrimination must come from a binder reading the P6 region and its conformational switch - which is exactly what the natural JDI TCR does.

## 2. TCRm antibodies and biologics against pMHC neoantigens

TP53 R175H (Hsiue 2021; note: this is NOT a KRAS target - see correction in Gaps):
- Hsiue EH, et al. Targeting a neoantigen derived from a common TP53 mutation. Science 2021. DOI 10.1126/science.abc8697. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abc8697 | PMID 33649166 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8208645/ .
- Target: p53 R175H peptide HMTEVVRHC (residues 168-176) on HLA-A*02:01; about 700 copies per cell by mass spectrometry.
- Phage-derived clones H2 and H20; H2-scDb (bispecific single-chain diabody) bound R175H/HLA-A*02:01 with KD = 86 nM (kon 1.76e5 M-1 s-1) and showed no detectable binding to WT p53/HLA-A*02:01 even at very high concentrations, whereas H20 cross-reacted with WT at high concentration - an explicit affinity/specificity trade-off example.
- Structure of H2-Fab with p53 R175H/HLA-A*02:01 (PDB 6W51, 3.5 A): total buried surface 1173 A2 (heavy chain 644 A2, light chain 529 A2), of which 818 A2 is buried on HLA - i.e., this TCRm antibody contacts the HLA helices extensively. Later peptide-centric designs explicitly try to avoid this.
- The H2 bispecific lysed neoantigen-presenting cancer cells in vitro and in mice.

RAS mutants (Douglass 2021; MANAbodies):
- Douglass J, et al. Bispecific antibodies targeting mutant RAS neoantigens. Sci Immunol 2021;6(57):eabd5515. DOI 10.1126/sciimmunol.abd5515. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciimmunol.abd5515 | PMID 33649101 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8141259/ .
- Targets: KRAS G12V on HLA-A*03:01 (peptides VVVGAVGVGK and VVGAVGVGK) and Q61H/L/R on HLA-A*01:01 (ILDTAGxEEY 10-mers). Phage clones: V2 (G12V), H1 (Q61H), L2 (Q61L), R6 (Q61R).
- V2 scFv: KD 0.28 nM for G12V-pHLA-A3, no appreciable WT binding; reformatted V2-U scDb: KD 24 nM, EC50 140 pM (IFN-gamma) and 76 pM (cytotoxicity), T-cell activation with as little as 10 pM peptide. L2-U scDb: 65 nM. H1-U and R6-U also showed no WT cross-reactivity but required 100x more peptide (100 nM) for activation.
- Antigen density: cells pulsed with 320 nM G12V peptide presented only about 300-800 antibody binding sites per cell; Q61H/L/R were presented at 583/512/127 copies per cell. Of six bispecific formats tested, the single-chain diabody (scDb) performed best at such low densities.

Tebentafusp (approved TCRm-style therapeutic; TCR-based, not an antibody):
- Format: ImmTAC = affinity-enhanced soluble TCR fused to anti-CD3 scFv; targets a gp100-derived peptide (YLEPGPVTA) on HLA-A*02:01 (peptide/allele cited in the Liu 2025 target list, https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.28.625793v1).
- Regulatory: FDA approval January 2022, EMA April 2022, for uveal melanoma (IJMS 2026 review, DOI 10.3390/ijms27198859, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13664799/).
- Pivotal phase 3 (Nathan P, et al. N Engl J Med 2021. DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2103485. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2103485 | PMID 34551229): 1-year overall survival 73% vs 59% (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.37-0.71, P<0.001); PFS 31% vs 19% at 6 months.
- Affinity: ImmTAC platform TCRs are engineered to picomolar affinity (Liddy N, et al. Monoclonal TCR-redirected tumor cell killing. Nat Med 2012. DOI 10.1038/nm.2764. https://doi.org/10.1038/nm.2764 | PMID 22561687 - each ImmTAC comprises a tumor-epitope-specific monoclonal TCR with picomolar affinity fused to a humanized anti-CD3 scFv).
- Affinity tuning: strong-affinity TCR plus intermediate-affinity anti-CD3 is optimal; very stable target-bispecific-effector complexes push CD3 into a non-signalling dark state (bioRxiv 2022.11.03.511676, https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.11.03.511676).

Other clinical-stage TCRm/TCR-biologic landscape (IJMS 2026 review table, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13664799/): tebentafusp (approved); brenetafusp/IMC-F106C (PRAME, phase III); IMC-P115C (PRAME, I/II); IMC-R117C (PIWIL1, I/II); IMCnyeso (NY-ESO-1, I/II); IMA402 (MAGEA4, I/II); CLSP-1025 (MAGEA4/A8, I); IMC-I109V (HBV, I); CorEngager CRPA1A2 (MAGEA1, IND). Most are TCR-based bispecifics (TCR-TCEs) rather than antibody-based TCRms. Antibody-format TCRm examples: Pr20 vs PRAME peptide/HLA-A2, KD about 4-5 nM, afucosylated form mediated ADCC in xenografts (Chang AY, et al. J Clin Invest 2017. DOI 10.1172/JCI96860. https://www.jci.org/articles/view/92335 | PMID 28862643); ESKM vs WT1/HLA-A*02 (Blood 2014. DOI 10.1182/blood-2014-01-549022. https://doi.org/10.1182/blood-2014-01-549022 | PMID 24723681). Reviews: Expert Opin Biol Ther 2016 (DOI 10.1080/14712598.2016.1176138) and Crit Rev Oncol Hematol 2026 (DOI 10.1016/j.critrevonc.2026.105673 | PMID 42946278) - the latter names the extremely low density of pMHC complexes on tumor cells as the primary challenge for all TCRm platforms.

KRAS G12D on the other allele (HLA-C*08:02) - closest published TCRm-antibody prior art:
- Discovery of TCR-like antibodies to the KRAS G12D neoantigen via in silico-in vitro workflow. Mol Ther 2026. DOI 10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.05.032. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymthe.2026.05.032 | PMID 42237541 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13555579/ | preprint https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.31.673313v1 .
- Antibodies against KRAS(G12D)10/HLA-C*08:02 generated by docking plus CDR-focused design and yeast-display maturation: initial Fab C8K10D KD about 1.36 uM (BLI); matured clones C8K10D5-1 and C8K10D5-18 at 5 nM and 27 nM (BLI; about 20 nM for three clones; about 70-fold improvement). Exclusive binding to the mutant complex; comprehensive phage-display specificity profiling showed no human off-target reactivity; active as CAR and T-cell engager.
- Clinical note: NT-112, a TCR-engineered T-cell therapy against KRAS(G12D)10/HLA-C*08:02, is in a phase I/II trial (NCT06218914), following durable responses in two advanced pancreatic cancer patients treated under compassionate use (same preprint).

KRAS G12V on HLA-A*11:01 AND HLA-A*03:01 (closest prior art on the SAME allele):
- Engineering T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies targeting KRAS G12V neoantigen across HLA-A*11:01 and HLA-A*03:01. J Immunother Cancer 2026. DOI 10.1136/jitc-2026-016411. https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2026-016411 | PMID 42896875 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13691759/ .
- Phage-derived scFv2 selectively recognized KRAS G12V/HLA-A*11:01 over WT and other KRAS mutants; scDb2 bound G12V/HLA-A*11:01 with KD 285 nM (SPR) and gave IFN-gamma EC50 0.59 nM on C1R-A11 cells; the same binder worked across HLA-A*11:01 and HLA-A*03:01, with estimated patient coverage about 5-6% in pancreatic and about 2% in colorectal cancer. Demonstrates that one TCRm paratope can read the same peptide on two different HLA alleles.

How single-residue discrimination is achieved (synthesis, all cited above):
- Direct burial of the mutant side chain with shape complementarity (H2 vs R175H: 86 nM, no WT binding at high concentration; structure 6W51).
- Indirect, electrostatic discrimination without direct mutant contacts (JDI TCR: near-identical structures, enthalpy-driven >4000-fold affinity window).
- Specificity actively maintained during affinity maturation (Poole: libraries depleted against WT before selection on G12D; only the most specific variants combined).
- Composite surfaces: antibodies often bury substantial HLA surface (H2: 818 A2 on HLA), which can confer selectivity when the mutant residue is clamped (Asp6-Arg114), but is also the source of off-target risk - the reason newer designs are peptide-centric.

## 3. De novo computational design of pMHC binders (2023-2026)

Correction of a common mis-citation: the Motmaen PNAS 2023 paper is a specificity-prediction method, not a minibinder-design paper. Exact citation: Motmaen A, Dauparas J, Baek M, Abedi MH, Baker D, Bradley P. Peptide-binding specificity prediction using fine-tuned protein structure prediction networks. PNAS 2023;120(9):e2216697120. DOI 10.1073/pnas.2216697120. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216697120 | PMID 36802421 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9992841/ . Content: AlphaFold fine-tuned with a classification head on 10,340 peptide-MHC examples across 68 Class I alleles, approaching NetMHCpan accuracy and generalizing to PDZ/SH3 domains; it highlights divergences between sequence- and structure-based predictions for 10-mer peptides, with the largest rank errors at the canonical anchors P2 and P10. Motmaen is a co-author of the actual pMHC binder design paper (Liu 2025), and this fine-tuned model is the specificity filter used by the design pipelines.

Liu et al. 2025 (the main de novo pMHC binder paper):
- Liu B, Greenwood NF, Bonzanini JE, Motmaen A, ... Garcia KC, Baker D. Design of high-specificity binders for peptide-MHC-I complexes. Science 2025. DOI 10.1126/science.adv0185. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adv0185 | PMID 40705892 | preprint https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.11.28.625793v1 .
- Method: RFdiffusion de novo design of small proteins that arc above the peptide binding groove and make extensive contacts with the peptide, explicitly avoiding extensive contacts with the ubiquitous MHC carrier; ProteinMPNN for sequence design; a fine-tuned AF2 model predicts on-target vs off-target binding (the Motmaen approach); yeast display screening of 200-12,000 designs per target; selection against 2-4 closely related proteome-derived peptides on the same HLA.
- Results: specific binders for 11 target pMHCs (published; ten in the preprint) across HLA-A*01:01, A*02:01, A*03:01. Targets include viral epitopes (SARS-CoV1 GLMWLSYFV, YFV LLWNGPIAV, HIV KLTPLCVTL), tumor antigens (WT1 RMFPNAPYL, PAP TLMSAMTNL, gp100 YLEPGPVTA, Mart-1 A2L ELAGIGILTV, PRAME ALYVDSLFFL), the CTNNB1 S5F neoantigen (TTAPFLSGK), and MAGE-A3 (EVDPIGHLY) with the Titin peptide (ESDPIVAQY) as off-target control. For 7/10 targets de novo diffusion was used, for 3/10 partial diffusion from an existing scaffold. CAR/Jurkat activation for 8 targets (published; 5 in preprint). SPR KD values of 96 nM and 35 nM for purified designs.
- Limitations: no experimental structures of the design-pMHC complexes (AF3/Chai-1 predictions matched the design models instead); some designs gave weak or background activation (MAGE-282 weak; hit-4 background attributed to other proteome peptides on the same HLA); no A*11:01 target and no KRAS target included.

Householder et al. 2025 (peptide-centric de novo TCRm, with experimental structure):
- Householder KD, Xiang X, Jude KM, ... Garcia KC. De novo design and structure of a peptide-centric TCR mimic binding module. Science 2025. DOI 10.1126/science.adv3813. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adv3813 | PMID 40705894 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12313176/ (bioRxiv preprint 14 Apr 2025).
- de novo alpha-helical TCRm against NY-ESO-1 peptide/HLA-A*02 with KD = 9.5 nM; crystal structure of the complex at 2.05 A shows rigid TCR-like docking with an unusual degree of focus on the up-facing NY-ESO-1 side chains.
- Specificity: a structure-informed in silico screen of 14,363 HLA-A*02 peptides correctly predicted two off-target peptides; the TCRm nevertheless maintained peptide selectivity and T-cell-engager cytotoxicity - an explicit, quantified statement that cross-reactivity risk is real but small.
- Method: RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN restricted to alpha-helical bundles (50 monomer folds of 80-100 aa generated; a 99-aa scaffold selected for RFdiffusion fold-conditioning). Motivation quoted from the paper: engineered TCRm antibodies can interact more prominently with the MHC helices than the peptide, leading to off-target toxicity.

Johansen et al. 2025 (rapid minibinder platform):
- Johansen KH, Wolff DS, Scapolo B, et al. De novo-designed pMHC binders facilitate T cell-mediated cytotoxicity toward cancer cells. Science 2025. DOI 10.1126/science.adv0422. https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adv0422 | PMID 40705893.
- Rapid de novo minibinder (miBd) platform using generative models; in silico cross-panning as a computational specificity prescreen; molecular dynamics to improve in vitro predictability. Produced a high-affinity binder to NY-ESO-1 peptide SLLMWITQC/HLA-A*02:01 (cryo-EM structure) and a binder to the neoantigen RVTDESILSY/HLA-A*01:01 (unknown structure); the NY-ESO-1 binder as a CAR killed NY-ESO-1-positive melanoma cells. KD value not in the public abstract (Science full text paywalled - gap).

2026 follow-ups:
- A generalizable system for antigenic peptide targeting across HLA-I allotypes. bioRxiv 2026-05-22. DOI 10.64898/2026.05.21.726655. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.21.726655 . Combines the peptide conformation predictor PepPred with the cross-HLA binder engineering platform TRACeR-I1 to build xTRACeRs compatible across HLA supertypes while maintaining peptide specificity; cryo-EM structures for a PRAME oncofetal antigen and a PHOX2B neuroblastoma peptide; CAR-T killing demonstrated. Directly relevant precedent for peptide-specific binders that navigate HLA polymorphism.
- Computational Design of a TCR-Based Bispecific Engager Targeting Cancerous KRAS G12V Mutations. ACS Omega 2026. DOI 10.1021/acsomega.5c10229. https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.5c10229 | PMID 41908412. In silico design of two TCR-BiTEs against KRAS G12V on HLA-A*03:01 and HLA-A*11:01, presented as starting points for further evaluation (no experimental binding data in the abstract).
- RFdiffusion3: De novo Design of All-atom Biomolecular Interactions with RFdiffusion3. bioRxiv 2025-09-18. DOI 10.1101/2025.09.18.676967. https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.18.676967v1 ; released December 2025 (https://www.ipd.uw.edu/2025/12/rfdiffusion3-now-available/). Its abstract covers ligand/nucleic-acid conditioning, enzyme design, DNA-binding proteins - no published pMHC application found (gap). Original RFdiffusion: Watson JL, et al. Nature 2023. DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06415-8. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06415-8 .

Reported failures and limitations across this literature:
- Design from docking alone is weak: the KRAS G12D/HLA-C*08:02 antibody workflow started at KD about 1.36 uM and needed yeast-display maturation to reach about 5-27 nM.
- No experimental structures for the Liu 2025 design complexes; several designs showed weak or background activation; screening burden is high (200-12,000 designs per target).
- Cross-reactivity is measurable: 2 predicted off-targets from 14,363 HLA-A*02 peptides (Householder); weak human mimetics RASL10A 460 nM and DNHD1 2.3 uM for the JDI TCR (Poole); explicit off-target toxicity risk when binders contact MHC helices more than the peptide (Householder).
- Empty/peptide-free MHC counterscreen is a real constraint: the published successful strategy is to minimize MHC-framework contacts (arc above the groove).

## 4. Target biology relevant to designing

HLA-A*11:01 peptide-binding motif:
- Anchors are P2 and the C-terminus. For HLA-A*11:01 specifically: C-terminal Lys, and at P2 Val, Ile, Phe, Tyr, Thr, with Thr/Val/Ile/Leu stronger than Phe/Tyr; hydrophobic P3 residues (Leu, Phe, Tyr, Ile, Ala) are secondary anchors (The role of anchor residues in the binding of peptides to HLA-A*1101 molecules, Tissue Antigens 1998. DOI 10.1111/j.1399-0039.1998.tb03080.x. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1399-0039.1998.tb03080.x ).
- Pool-sequencing originals: Falk K, et al. Peptide motifs of HLA-A1, -A11, -A31, and -A33 molecules. Immunogenetics 1994. DOI 10.1007/BF00167086. https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00167086 | PMID 8039832 ; and Definition of specific peptide motifs for four major HLA-A alleles. J Immunol 1994. DOI 10.4049/jimmunol.152.8.3913. https://doi.org/10.4049/jimmunol.152.8.3913 (motifs 9-10 aa, anchors at P2 and the C-terminus; HLA-A3 and A11 motifs very similar).
- For 10-mers the anchors sit at P2 and P10 (Motmaen 2023: canonical anchor positions 2 and 10 for a 10-mer peptide).
- Peptide VVVGADGVGK: P2 = V (strong anchor), P10 = K (canonical C-terminal anchor), P6 = D/G at the center (the mutated position).

Why P6 is central and mobile for this 10-mer:
- In the apo A*11:01 complexes, residues 4-6 form the central bulge; in 7OW4 (G12D, no TCR) P5-P6 are not modeled at all (disordered; verified from deposited coordinates); on TCR binding, P6 switches from an exposed upward position to buried (Poole 2022). The center of the 10-mer is the most conformationally labile part of the epitope and the part used for discrimination.

Peptide-free (empty) MHC stability:
- Empty MHC class I molecules can assemble but are unstable at body temperature: RMA-S cells at 19-33 C accumulate peptide-free H-2Kb/b2m complexes at the surface; these are labile at 37 C and are stabilized by specific peptides (Ljunggren HG, et al. Empty MHC class I molecules come out in the cold. Nature 1990. DOI 10.1038/346476a0. https://doi.org/10.1038/346476a0 | PMID 2198471).
- Empty heterodimers can be prepared biochemically from denatured heavy and light chains and will bind peptides (Fahnestock et al. Science 1992, cited in: Effects of peptide length and composition on binding to an empty class I MHC heterodimer. Biochemistry 1994. DOI 10.1021/bi00192a020. https://doi.org/10.1021/bi00192a020).
- Challenge requirement: designs must not bind peptide-free HLA-A*11:01/b2m, measured under the same conditions (challenge page). Implication from the literature: the empty target is structurally compromised and sticky surfaces are a real risk; the design rule supported by the cited work is to avoid HLA-framework contacts and focus on the peptide and groove.
- Gap: no quantitative half-life or thermostability for peptide-free HLA-A*11:01 specifically was found.

Off-target landscape:
- The germline WT KRAS peptide VVVGAGGVGK is itself presented on HLA-A*11:01 (apo structure 7OW3; TCR complex 7OW5). It is the primary counterscreen target and a self-peptide.
- Human self-peptidome mimetics of the G12D epitope: JDI TCR profiling found only RASL10A (460 nM) and DNHD1 (2.3 uM) among 10 candidates; DNHD1 gave about 12% of the on-target response in cells (Poole 2022).
- Systematic off-target screening approaches: designs screened against 2-4 proteome-derived closely related peptides per target on the same HLA (Liu 2025); large-scale in silico peptide screens (Householder 2025: 14,363 HLA-A*02 peptides). Benign immunopeptidome references: HLA Ligand Atlas https://hla-ligand-atlas.org (Marcu A, et al. J Immunother Cancer 2021, https://jitc.bmj.com/content/9/4/e002071 ; DIA extension 2025, DOI 10.1136/jitc-2025-012083) and IEDB https://www.iedb.org .

HLA-A*11:01 population frequency:
- Chinese population table: HLA-A*11:01 18.57% - the most frequent HLA-A allele in that table, ahead of A*24:02 (15.73%) and A*02:01 (13.79%). Source: Table 1 of PMC7926959 (Viruses 2021, article v13-00333), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7926959/table/viruses-13-00333-t001/ .
- AFND (Allele Frequency Net Database) lists A*11:01 as reported in 281 populations (search-result URL https://allelefrequencies.net/hla6002a.asp?all_name=A*11:01 ; all AFND query URLs I tried returned HTTP 404, so no AFND numeric range is quoted - treat AFND as the canonical source to verify).

## 5. Assay and quantitative expectations

What the challenge measures (all from the official challenge page, https://proteinbase.com/competitions/anthropic-adaptyv-2026/challenges/pmhc):
- Three targets measured under the same conditions: KRAS G12D complex, WT KRAS complex, and peptide-free HLA-A*11:01 with b2m; with the design immobilised and the target in solution.
- Binding definition, quoted: A design counts as binding a target when its sensorgram gives a fittable curve and a KD, or, where the curve cannot be fitted, when the association shift clears 300% over the negative control.
- Ranking: (1) mutation specificity - measurable KD against G12D and no detectable binding to WT; (2) peptide-dependent binding - measurable KD against G12D and no detectable binding to peptide-free HLA; (3) affinity - lowest KD against G12D. Designs binding both WT and G12D rank after specific ones by the KD ratio (WT/G12D); likewise for peptide-free. Structure reference: 7OW6 chains A-C with TCR removed. Submissions close Sunday 8 November 2026, 23:59 AoE.

What no detectable binding means in SPR/BLI practice (from the cited papers):
- Reported as NB (no binding) when no response distinguishable from control is observed at the highest tested concentration (Poole 2022 Table 1: WT, G12C, ERAS, RRAS, MRAS, DIRAS2, RAP1A all NB).
- No detectable binding even at very high scDb concentrations (Hsiue 2021); no appreciable binding (Douglass 2021); no measurable binding to a control pMHC (KRAS Ab preprint). Operationally: a binder that only engages WT at very high concentration (like H20-scDb) fails such counterscreens.

Typical affinities by modality (all cited above):
- Native/patient TCRs: weak - JDI 63 uM; nanomolar to low micromolar for the therapeutic C*08:02 TCRs; natural TCRs generally recognize pHLA with very weak affinities (Crean RM, et al. Molecular Rules Underpinning Enhanced Affinity Binding of Human T Cell Receptors Engineered for Immunotherapy. Mol Ther Oncolytics 2020. DOI 10.1016/j.omto.2020.07.008. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.omto.2020.07.008 | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7452143/).
- Engineered TCRs: picomolar (63.7-743 pM, Poole; picomolar, Liddy 2012); enhancements up to several million-fold without changing binding mode, often via buried hydrophobic residues (Crean 2020).
- TCRm antibodies: 0.28-86 nM (V2 scFv 0.28 nM; H2 86 nM; Pr20 4-5 nM; KRAS G12D/C*08:02 antibodies 5-27 nM).
- De novo pMHC minibinders: 9.5 nM (Householder, with a 2.05 A structure); 35-96 nM (Liu SPR examples).
- Bispecific formats tolerate weaker KD: V2-U scDb 24 nM vs V2 scFv 0.28 nM; G12V scDb2 285 nM still functional (cellular EC50 0.59 nM).

Hit rates and design success:
- Ordinary protein targets: de novo binder design success is low and AF2/RoseTTAFold filtering increases success rates nearly 10-fold (Bennett NR, et al. Improving de novo protein binder design with deep learning. Nat Commun 2023. DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-38328-5. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-38328-5 | PMID 37149653); newer methods report 3- to 300-fold better affinities and higher experimental success rates on seven target proteins (AlphaProteo technical report, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08022).
- For pMHC targets: Liu 2025 screened 200-12,000 designs per target on yeast and obtained specific binders for 10/10 targets (preprint; 11 published), with CAR function for 5/10 (preprint; 8 published). No per-design hit-rate percentages were found in any published pMHC binder paper (gap). Practical benchmark: screen hundreds to thousands; expect binders, but expect few.
- Largest public triage dataset: meta-analysis of 3,766 experimentally tested de novo binders across 15 targets (bioRxiv 2025.08.14.670059, https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.08.14.670059); AF3-derived interface metrics (ipSAE) predict experimental success best.
- Affinity ceilings: no hard ceiling reported; picomolar is achievable for TCRs and low nM for de novo minibinders against pMHC. Bispecifics work at 24-285 nM, but this challenge ranks by lowest KD for a direct-binding readout, so tighter is strictly better.

## What this implies for design strategy (each bullet grounded in the cited findings)

1. Center the design on the P4-P7 region and the P6 hotspot; avoid flat HLA-only surfaces. The challenge recommends targeting the peptide and the groove around it, the surface a T-cell receptor reads (challenge page); the structures show the P4-P6 bulge and the P6 switch (Poole 2022; 7OW4/7OW6).
2. Exploit the Asp6 clamp. The natural discrimination solution is burial of Asp6 against Arg114/Gln70 (salt bridge 2.95 A, H-bond 3.02 A [computed from 7OW6]) - impossible for Gly6. A binder presenting a basic group (Arg/Lys) toward the Asp6 carboxylate, or one that stabilizes the R114-clamped peptide conformation, is directly motivated by the mechanism (Poole 2022). Note the free peptide prefers the bulged/exposed state (7OW4 P5-P6 disordered), so the design may need to induce the buried conformation.
3. Minimize HLA-framework contacts to pass the peptide-free counterscreen and the ranking. The published successful strategy is to arc above the peptide-binding groove and make extensive peptide contacts (Liu 2025), and to be peptide-centric because antibody TCRms that touch MHC helices more than peptide carry off-target toxicity risk (Householder 2025).
4. Build specificity screening in from the start: fine-tuned AlphaFold specificity models (Motmaen 2023) plus in silico screens against the WT peptide and proteome-derived close matches (Liu 2025); even a 14,363-peptide screen still predicted 2 off-targets (Householder 2025), so plan counter-screens.
5. Expect low per-design success and screen deeply: 200-12,000 designs per target on yeast (Liu 2025); low base success rates for ordinary targets (Bennett 2023); higher rates achievable with newer methods on some targets (AlphaProteo 2024).
6. Aim for the tightest affinity you can measure: the challenge ranks by lowest KD and defines success by a fittable KD or a 300% association shift (challenge page). Bispecific/avidity formats can rescue weak intrinsic binders (Douglass 2021: 0.28 nM scFv vs 24 nM scDb; JITC 2026: 285 nM scDb still functional), but this is a direct-binding BLI-style assay, so intrinsic affinity is what counts.
7. Design for both peptide conformational states: check models against the exposed bulged state (apo structures 7OW3/7OW4 - likely closer to the free recombinant complex) and the buried state (TCR-bound 7OW5/7OW6). A rigid binder assuming the buried state may fail on a free complex that needs induced fit (Poole 2022; 7OW4 disorder).
8. Cross-reactivity risk is real but quantifiable: published weak mimetics are 100-1000x weaker than on-target (RASL10A 460 nM, DNHD1 2.3 uM vs pM-range on-target), and 2 predicted off-targets out of 14,363 peptides were tolerated by a therapeutic TCRm (Poole 2022; Householder 2025).

## Gaps and unknowns

- No published de novo binder or TCRm antibody against KRAS G12D/HLA-A*11:01 specifically.
